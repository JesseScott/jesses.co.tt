Meanwhile: Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 8, 2026

Meanwhile is a free Android app by Jesse Scott (British Columbia, Canada). It finds the point on Earth exactly opposite you and shows news from that country, or, when that point is open ocean, the sea conditions there. It is a hobby project: it has no accounts, no ads, and no servers of its own. This page explains what the app does with information, so you can decide whether you are comfortable with it.

The short version

  • Meanwhile asks for approximate (coarse) location only, to work out the point opposite you. It does not store your location and does not send it to me; I run no server that could receive it.
  • The app has no accounts, no ads and does not sell or share your data for advertising.
  • Usage and crash reports are off unless you turn them on in the app. They are anonymous, they include the country of the headlines you load but never your exact location or the place you search for, and you can turn them off again at any time.
  • Some information necessarily goes to the services that supply the headlines, sea data and place lookups. They are listed below.

Like any app that uses the internet, a request to one of these services reveals your device's IP address and basic technical details to that service. Meanwhile does not add anything to identify you to them.

Usage and crash reports (optional, off by default)

If you turn on “Share usage and crash data” (in the app's About screen, or when it asks you after it has worked a few times), Meanwhile uses Firebase, a Google service, to see how the app is used and to find crashes. Meanwhile can only send facts from a fixed list:

  • which screens were opened (About, open source licenses, the support link), and whether you chose a place by using your location or by searching (not which place);
  • whether a load worked, how long it took (roughly), roughly how many headlines it found, which news source supplied them (not which articles), and which kind of error happened if it failed;
  • the country of the headlines you load, as a two-letter country code (for example “NZ”), whether you used your location or searched for a place. When you are looking at the open ocean, no country is sent;
  • whether you allowed the location permission, and which view (land or ocean) you switched to;
  • crash reports, containing the type of error and where in the code it happened, but not error messages;
  • your phone's model and Android version, the app's version, and a random installation ID (a number that is not tied to you and changes if you reinstall).

The app is built so that these reports do not include your exact location, the coordinates of the point opposite you, the place you searched for, or the headlines you read. The country of the headlines is usually on the far side of the world from you, so it hints at roughly where you are; treat it as coarse location data. It is never attached to the report that says an article was opened. Reports started to include the country in build 9 (October 2026); if you had answered the app's question about reports before then, the app asks again and sends nothing until you answer. The advertising ID is not collected and ad personalization is turned off. If you turn the switch off again, reports stop, the app resets its random installation ID and discards reports it had not yet sent. Reports already sent stay with Firebase until its retention period ends. Google processes this data under its own terms; see the Firebase link above.

Links that leave the app

Tapping a headline opens the publisher's own website in your browser. The support link opens Ko-fi in your browser. Those sites have their own privacy policies, and Meanwhile has no control over them.

Who else handles information

  • Google: location (Google Play services), place lookups (device geocoder) and, only if you opt in, Firebase.
  • GDELT Project: the headlines.
  • Open-Meteo: sea conditions, used under CC BY 4.0 for non-commercial purposes.
  • Public news feeds from some outlets, for a few small places, such as RNZ and Matangi Tonga.

Keeping and deleting information

I hold no personal information about you: there is no account or server. Information on your phone (the headline cache and settings) is removed when you clear the app's storage or uninstall it. Turning off usage and crash reports stops further reports and resets the installation ID, so later reports can't be linked to earlier ones. Reports already sent are held by Firebase for its retention period; to ask about those, or anything else, contact me (below).

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in Europe, or PIPEDA and British Columbia's privacy law in Canada), you may have rights to access, correct or delete personal information, and to withdraw consent. Because Meanwhile keeps no personal information on a server of its own, most of that is in your hands on your phone, and you can withdraw consent for reports at any time with the switch in the app. If you want to exercise a right, contact me.

Children

Meanwhile is not designed for children, and I do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 13.

Security

The app talks to its services over encrypted connections (HTTPS). No method of transmission or storage is perfectly secure, but there is no database of user information for anyone to break into.

Changes to this policy

If the app starts doing something different with information, I will update this page and the “Last updated” date, and the app's About screen will say the same thing. The policy is also available from the app's Play Store listing.
